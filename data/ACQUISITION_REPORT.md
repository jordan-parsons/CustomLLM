**File formats (`PRIMARY`, read from the files):**

- `*.edge` — DIMACS-like. First line `p edge <n> <m>`; then `m` lines `e <a> <b>`, **1-indexed**,

  ```
  {0, 0}
  {1, 0}
  {-1/2, Sqrt[3]/2}
  ```

  Line *k* is vertex *k* of the `.edge` file. Radicals used: `Sqrt[2,3,5,6,11,15,33,55,165,11/3,2/3,3/2,5/3]`.
- `cnf/*.cnf` — 4-colorability CNF encodings (`-sbp` = with symmetry-breaking predicates).
- `proof/*.drat` — DRAT unsatisfiability proofs.
- `check/*.singular` — Singular scripts (Gröbner basis) proving the unit-distance property
  exactly; generated by `check/check_dist_one.py <x.vtx> <x.edge>`.

Other CNP-SAT contents: `cnf/517-4.edge` is a *CNF*-format file despite the name (`p cnf 2068 13935`)

## 2. de Grey's construction (arXiv:1804.02385)

### 2.1 H — **CONFIRMED**

**H = the centre plus the 6 vertices of a regular hexagon of side 1.** 7 vertices, **12 unit edges**
(6 spokes + 6 hexagon sides). `VERIFIED` computationally; the description is corroborated by a
MathWorld snippet ("H consists of the centre and vertices of a regular hexagon of side-length 1").
Your belief was correct.

Equivalently: H is a point of the unit triangular (Eisenstein) lattice together with its 6 nearest
neighbours.

### 2.2 J (31 vertices) — **CONFIRMED and given an exact closed form**

I `VERIFIED` an exact characterisation. Write lattice points in Eisenstein coordinates
`(a,b) ↦ a + b·ω` with `ω = exp(iπ/3)`, so `|a+bω|² = a² + ab + b²`. Then

> **J = { (a,b) ∈ ℤ² : a² + ab + b² ≤ 7 }**

Computationally confirmed properties (`verify_scripts/lattice2.py`, exact integer arithmetic):

- `|J| = 31` ✓
- norm distribution `{0:1, 1:6, 3:6, 4:6, 7:12}` — i.e. shells at distance `0, 1, √3, 2, √7`
- **72 unit edges**
- J contains **exactly 13 copies of H**, centred at norms `0` (1 copy), `1` (6 copies), `3` (6 copies)
  — i.e. **at distances 0, 1 and √3 from the centre**
- **the union of those 13 copies is exactly J** ✓

This reproduces the literature description ("J contains a copy of H in its centre, six copies centred
at distance 1, and six copies centred at distance √3", `SNIPPET` from MathWorld) *and* proves the
union is consistent — the 13 copies are all in the **same lattice orientation** (no rotation at this
stage). High confidence.

*(Caution during reconstruction: a first attempt using floating-point string keys gave 43 instead of
31, purely from `-0.0` vs `0.0` key mismatches. The exact-integer redo gives 31. If downstream code
dedupes points by stringified floats, it will silently over-count.)*

### 2.3 K (61) and L (121) — structure confirmed, **exact angles NOT FOUND**

`SNIPPET` (MathWorld): "K is constructed as the union of J with a copy of J rotated clockwise around
the origin"; L likewise from two copies of K.

What I could `VERIFIED` confirm — **the vertex arithmetic only**:

- `K = J ∪ rot_θ(J)` about the **origin** gives `|K| = 31 + 31 − 1 = 61` ✓ (only the origin is shared)
- `L = K ∪ rot_φ(K)` about the origin gives `|L| = 61 + 61 − 1 = 121` ✓

**Critical caveat: the vertex counts 61 and 121 do NOT pin down θ and φ.** Any rotation in general
position gives 61 and 121, because only the fixed origin coincides. I tested θ, φ ∈
{arccos(5/6), arcsin(1/(2√3)), 2·arcsin(1/4)} and several combinations all yield |K|=61 and |L|=121,
differing only in edge count (L had 300 or 312 unit pairs depending on the pair chosen).

**Therefore: the exact rotation angles defining K and L are `NOT FOUND`.** One snippet claimed "the
two copies of K are created with a relative rotation of 2·arcsin(1/4)" — I rate this **LOW
confidence** and **partly contradicted** by my field analysis (§2.5): `2·arcsin(1/4)` requires √15,
i.e. √5, which does **not** occur in de Grey-lineage coordinates. Do not build on it.

### 2.5 Coordinate field and rotation angles — **CONFIRMED, with an important addition**

Your hypothesis is **correct for de Grey**, and **incomplete for the small record-adjacent graphs.**

**de Grey lineage: ℚ(√3, √11).** `VERIFIED` from primary data — the de Grey-lineage coordinate files
(`S199.vtx`, `L403.vtx`, `G2167.vtx`) contain **only** `Sqrt[3], Sqrt[11], Sqrt[33], Sqrt[11/3]`,
all of which lie in ℚ(√3,√11) (`√33 = √3·√11`). Corroborated by a `SNIPPET` stating de Grey works
with coordinates in `(ℚ[√3,√11])²`.

I extracted the **rotation spectrum** empirically (`verify_scripts/lattice2.py`): for every rotation
about the origin taking one data point to another of equal modulus, I counted how many points map
back into the set. Results:

| rotation | value | cos, sin | maps points back into set |
|---|---|---|---|
| lattice symmetry | 60°, 120°, 180°, 240°, 300° | `cos=±1/2` | 2105/2167 (G2167), 181/199 (S199) |
| **θ₀ = arcsin(1/(2√3))** | **≈ 16.7787°** | `cos θ₀ = √33/6`, `sin θ₀ = 1/(2√3)` | **689/2167, 51/199, 144/403** — the dominant non-trivial rotation |
| **α = 2θ₀ = arccos(5/6)** | **≈ 33.5573°** | `cos α = 5/6`, `sin α = √11/6` | 25/199, 66/510 |
| β = 2·arcsin(1/4) | ≈ 28.955° | `cos β = 7/8`, `sin β = √15/8` | **1/199** (absent from de Grey lineage) |

So:

- **Your α is CONFIRMED**: `cos α = 5/6`, `sin α = √11/6`, α = arccos(5/6) ≈ 33.5573°. `VERIFIED`
- **Refinement:** the *atomic* rotation actually present in the data is the **half-angle**
  θ₀ = α/2 = arcsin(1/(2√3)) ≈ 16.7787°, with `cos θ₀ = √33/6`. It maps far more points back into
  the set than α does. This is why `Sqrt[33]` and `Sqrt[11/3]` are so frequent in the `.vtx` files.
  If you only implement α you will miss half the geometry. `VERIFIED`
- **Geometric role of α (`VERIFIED` arithmetic):** rotating a point at distance **√3** from the
  centre by α moves it a chord of `2·√3·sin(α/2) = 2√3 · 1/(2√3) = 1` — **exactly 1**. That is the
  spindling step that manufactures new unit edges, and it is exactly why the √3-shell of J matters
  and why √11 enters the field.
- **√3 is needed for the lattice**, as you supposed. `VERIFIED`

**CORRECTION / ADDITION — the small graphs need a third generator √5.** The record-adjacent graphs
510, 517, 529, 553, 610, 633, 803, 826, 874 contain `Sqrt[5], Sqrt[15], Sqrt[55], Sqrt[165], Sqrt[5/3]`
in addition. Their coordinate field is

> **ℚ(√3, √5, √11)** — degree 8 over ℚ (also containing √15, √33, √55, √165).

`VERIFIED` from the `.vtx` files, and corroborated `PRIMARY` by Heule's own Singular script
`check/553.singular`, whose ring is declared with generators `(r3, s11, s5, s3)` and ideal
`s11²−11, s5²−5, s3²−3, s3·r3−1`.

The extra generator comes from a **second spindling rotation**:
`β = 2·arcsin(1/4)`, `cos β = 7/8`, `sin β = √15/8` ≈ 28.955°, which moves a point at distance **2**
by a chord of `2·2·sin(β/2) = 1`. Empirically β maps **94/510** and **94/517** points back into
those sets, versus **1/199** for de Grey's S199. `VERIFIED`. So β is a Heule/Polymath-era addition,
**not** part of de Grey's original construction.

`Sqrt[2]`, `Sqrt[6]`, `Sqrt[2/3]`, `Sqrt[3/2]` occur **only** in `T721.vtx`, an unrelated family
(and T721 is 4-colorable). Ignore them for the plane construction.

**Practical recommendation:** implement exact arithmetic over ℚ(√3, √5, √11) (degree 8; e.g. as
ℚ-vectors on the basis `1, √3, √5, √11, √15, √33, √55, √165`). ℚ(√3,√11) suffices only if you
restrict to de Grey's original graphs and will *fail* on the 510–874 family.

## 4. Verification methodology

Scripts are preserved in `/home/user/CustomLLM/data/verify_scripts/`
(`verify.py`, `g2167.py`, `lattice2.py`, `kl.py`, `vdim.py`). Requires `sympy`, `mpmath`,
`python-sat`, `networkx` (`sympy`/`mpmath` were pip-installed this session).

1. **Exact unit-distance check.** Parsed each `.vtx` into sympy expressions (`Sqrt[x] → sqrt(x)`),
   and for every declared edge computed `(Δx)² + (Δy)²` symbolically and required
   `simplify(d² − 1) == 0`. Result: **0 non-unit edges across all 13 graphs.** No floating-point
   tolerance involved.
2. **Chromatic lower bound.** Built the 4-colouring CNF (one clause per vertex forcing a colour, and
   for each edge and colour a clause forbidding a monochromatic pair, plus a 2-clause symmetry break
   on the first edge) and ran **CaDiCaL 1.5.3** via `python-sat`. UNSAT ⇒ χ ≥ 5. All 9 graphs from
   510 to 874 are UNSAT; S199/L403/T721/G2167 are SAT.
3. **Consistency.** Declared `p edge n m` headers matched actual edge-line counts and max vertex
   index for all files; no duplicate points in G2167.
4. **Construction reconstruction.** Exact integer Eisenstein arithmetic for H and J; high-precision
   `mpmath` (40 dps) with exact sympy confirmation for the rotation spectrum and K/L counts.

### Caveats on what verification does and does not prove

- UNSAT proves **χ ≥ 5**; combined with χ(ℝ²) ≤ 7 and the published claims, these are 5-chromatic.
  I did **not** separately exhibit 5-colourings, so strictly I verified `χ ≥ 5`, not `χ = 5`.
- I verified the graphs are **subgraphs** of the unit-distance graph (every declared edge has exact
  distance 1). I did **not** check that every unit-distance pair is present as an edge — that is not
  required for a lower-bound witness, and Heule's files are deliberately edge-subsets in some cases.
- I re-derived proofs independently with my own solver; I did **not** replay Heule's supplied DRAT
  proofs. Those are available in `CNP-SAT/proof/` if independent proof-checking is wanted.
